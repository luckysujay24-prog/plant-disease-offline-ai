# Nutrition & Care Advisor: Agronomic Rules & Knowledge Base

## 1. Core Principles & Honesty Guarantees

1. **Fertilizer Does Not Cure Disease**:
   Plant nutrition provides essential elements for physiological vigor and cell wall strength, but cannot eradicate fungal, bacterial, or viral infections. The application explicitly discloses this on every advisory view.
2. **Precedence of Soil Testing**:
   No arbitrary numeric doses (kg/acre) are generated. The farmer is consistently instructed to follow their formal soil test report or consult their local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK).
3. **Zero Chemical Product Endorsements**:
   No commercial pesticide or fungicide brand names or chemical doses are included.

## 2. Weather Timing Rules (R1 to R8)

| Rule ID | Method | Weather Condition | Action | Rationale | Citation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **R1** | Soil | Rain $\ge 4.0$ mm or prob $\ge 50\%$ | **WAIT** | Surface runoff and deep nitrate leaching waste nutrients and contaminate waterways. | Havlin et al. (2014); ICAR Nutrient Stewardship |
| **R2** | Foliar | Rain within 6-12 hours | **WAIT** | Free precipitation washes foliar sprays off leaf cuticles prior to stomatal uptake. | Fageria et al. (2009), *J. Plant Nutrition* |
| **R3** | Foliar | Wind speed $\ge 18$ km/h | **AVOID** | Elevated wind velocity produces droplet drift and uneven canopy deposition. | FAO Ground Spray Guidelines (2001) |
| **R4** | Foliar | Temperature $\ge 33$°C | **AVOID** | Midday solar radiation combined with salt sprays induces severe osmotic leaf scorch. Spray early morning or evening. | Fernandez & Eichert (2009), *Crit. Rev. Plant Sci.* |
| **R5** | Soil | Waterlogged soil ($\ge 35$ mm rain) | **WAIT** | Anaerobic root zones suppress active root respiration and nutrient transport. | Marschner (2012), *Mineral Nutrition* |
| **R6** | Soil | Extended dry spell | **GOOD** | Apply with or prior to light irrigation to dissolve nutrients to root depth. | ICAR Handbook of Agriculture (6th Ed.) |
| **R7** | Both | Consecutive wet days with active fungal infection | **WAIT** | Excessive nitrogen top-dressing produces thin-walled, succulent foliage highly susceptible to hyphal ingress. | Huber & Graham (1999) |
| **R8** | Both | Favorable dry window ($<2$ mm rain, $<15$ km/h wind) | **GOOD** | Ambient conditions maximize physiological uptake without environmental loss. | IPNI 4R Nutrient Stewardship |
